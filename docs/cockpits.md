# Cockpit references and modeling notes

Control locations in the model use metres in the aircraft body frame. Mouse targets on moving controls use offsets from the corresponding mesh pivot. The lever positions and grip sizes are estimates from the panel layouts. The reference material does not establish surveyed cockpit dimensions.

| Configuration | Reference and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Cessna 172RG Cutlass, conventional panel | Section 7 identifies the carbureted engine, constant-speed propeller and four fuel feeds labeled LEFT, BOTH, RIGHT and OFF. The simulator pools the fuel and simplifies the magnetos to OFF/BOTH/START. The radio wheel tunes nearby stations. [Cessna 172RG operating handbook](https://sportysacademy-images.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/07170333/172RG-POH-from-Web.pdf) |
| Robin DR400/180 Régent | The handbook panel key, pp. 1.10–1.11, distinguishes the magneto selector (35) and starter button (44). It identifies the throttle (40), mixture (42), tank selector (43), trim wheel (45), parking brake (47) and flap lever (48). Instrument and radio fits vary by serial number. The simulated electrical bus has one master. [Robin DR400/180 flight manual](https://www.aeroclubduplandedieu.fr/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/MANUEL-DE-VOL-DR400-180-NS2062-a-2215.pdf) |
| Beechcraft A36 Bonanza (E-2111 on), 1990s steam-gauge panel | The shop manual describes the pull-to-set parking brake, the electric trim with its manual wheel, the single throw-over control column, the separate battery and alternator switches, the fuel selector on the floor at the left wing root, the cowl flap knob and the alternate air T-handle. From 1984 the A36 has the throttle, propeller and mixture quadrant, with the gear switch to its left and the flap switch (UP, APPR, DN) to its right. The Beech arrangement follows a study simulation's manual of the A36 panel: annunciators under the pilot's glareshield, engine gauges in a column right of the radio stack, fuel gauges over the quadrant, switches and climate controls on the left subpanel, the main breaker panel on the left sidewall and the avionics breakers on the right subpanel. Exact instrument, switch, radio and breaker positions are estimates from photographs. The selector offers LEFT, RIGHT and OFF; the simulator pools the fuel and has one master, so the alternator switch follows the battery switch. The magnetos are simplified to OFF/BOTH/START. [Beech 36/A36 shop manual](http://www.aeroelectric.com/Reference_Docs/Beech/beech_36_a36_a36tc_shop_manual_v1998.pdf) · [Aviation Consumer A36 review](https://aviationconsumer.com/?p=1984) · [Black Square Bonanza manual](https://downloads.justflight.com/products/JFF004080/BKSQ_BonanzaProfessionalManual.pdf) |
| Boeing 747-400 passenger flight deck | Boeing shows the battery pushbutton on the forward overhead and the four thrust levers and fuel controls on the pedestal. Reverse levers share one reverse command and the speedbrake uses the simulator’s deployment settings. The four fuel control switches and the four overhead engine start switches each act as one target driving the simulator’s single fuel control and starter; the switches themselves do not move. The parking brake T-handle position is an estimate. [Boeing 747-400 flight deck control locations, sheet 747.6.7](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/airports/arff/arff747.pdf) |
| Concorde, production British Airways flight deck | G-BOAG supplies the flight-deck layout reference. The four thrust levers sit on the pedestal. The nose and visor lever operates the simulator’s four existing detents. The engineer’s station and engine-specific controls remain simplified. [Museum of Flight Concorde and interior tour](https://www.museumofflight.org/Exhibits-and-Events/Aircraft/concorde) |
| F/A-18C, single-seat legacy Hornet | The C/D front cockpit foldout identifies the throttle quadrant and left-side controls. The simulator links both throttle levers and simplifies flap scheduling. [NATOPS cockpit foldouts](https://www.vmfa251.org/pdffiles/F18-ABCD-000.pdf) |
| F-4E, slatted Phantom | The pilot’s slats/flaps selector sits outboard of the left throttle, with three positions. The simulator maps these to its existing flap settings. Some seat, throttle grip and pedal geometry is shared with the Hornet. [F-4E flight manual](https://www.scribd.com/document/337169977/F-4E-Flight-Manual) |
| F-22A Raptor | The right-hand side-stick and left-console throttles follow the documented arrangement. There are two grips with a shared simulated throttle axis. Display pages and symbology still use the simulator’s Hornet implementation. [NASA cockpit description](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/643903main_breakingmishapchain-ebook.pdf) · [USAF museum cockpit photograph](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Upcoming/Photos/igphoto/2000412735/) |
| SR-71A Blackbird | The forward cockpit supplies the pilot view. The striped chute handle deploys or releases the simulator’s braking parachute. The rear reconnaissance systems officer’s equipment remains decorative. [USAF museum cockpit photograph](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Upcoming/Photos/igphoto/2000521423/) |

The front-panel and overhead legends, bezels and switches include representative details. Aircraft-specific avionics, independent engine management, tank-by-tank fuel consumption and full crew-station procedures are outside the current simulation.
